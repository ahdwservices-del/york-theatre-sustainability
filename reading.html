Waste

Key numbers

10waste streams in theatre, each with a different cost and carbon footprint.
60%of healthcare waste should be offensive (tiger) waste: the national target, with 20% alternative treatment and 20% incineration. (HTM 07-01, 2022)
Up to 50×more carbon per tonne when waste is incinerated (1,074 kg CO₂e) rather than recycled (21–65 kg CO₂e). (Rizan 2021)

Why the bin you choose matters

The same bag of waste can carry up to fifty times more carbon depending on which stream it goes into. Clean packaging in a clinical bag is the costliest mistake, in both carbon and money. Most packaging is opened before the patient arrives, so it is clean and can be recycled.

The Which bin? finder follows HTM 07-01 waste segregation for the anaesthetic room, theatre and recovery, and pharmaceutical waste guidance. Your organisation's waste policy and local bin labels take precedence.

Carbon per anaesthetic

For the same operation (operative hysteroscopy), one life cycle assessment estimated 5.5 kg CO₂e per case for spinal anaesthesia, 6.6 kg for TIVA and 10.8 kg for low-flow sevoflurane. (Lechani et al, Br J Anaesth 2026; single centre)

Planet health check

Explaining it to a colleague in 30 seconds

It's a blood panel for the planet

Nine tests, each with a reference range set by science. Inside the range, Earth stays in the stable state that let human civilisation develop. Seven results are now flagged abnormal; several are critical values.

The results interact

Like a patient's renal and cardiac function, the systems feed each other. The CO₂ that warms the climate also acidifies the ocean; forest loss worsens both climate and biodiversity. You can't treat one value in isolation.

Abnormal is not yet irreversible

A boundary is a precaution line, not a cliff edge, much like a reference range. Crossing it raises risk. The ozone layer shows a flagged result can recover when the world agrees a treatment plan and sticks to it.

How to read the wheel

Each slice's length shows how far a measure has moved from its pre-industrial state, scaled so the dashed ring is the boundary. Beyond the ring the scale is compressed so the largest overshoots fit. The short dark tick on a slice marks its high-risk line. A hatched slice with a question mark (novel entities) is judged breached but has no single number yet.

The 6 Rs

Why theatres?

The six steps run in order of impact. Rethink and refuse stop waste existing at all; reduce and reuse make what we use go further; recycling deals with what is left, and research tells us what works.

Operating theatres are among the most carbon-intensive parts of a hospital: on average about 188 tonnes of CO₂ equivalent per theatre each year in one three-country study, mostly from energy, anaesthetic gases and single-use items. (MacNeill 2017)

Explaining it to a colleague in 30 seconds

Prevention beats treatment

Rethink and refuse are the pre-op assessment: they stop the problem before it starts. Recycling is the resus team. It is vital, but by the time you need it the item has already been made, shipped, opened and thrown away.

Three questions before you open anything

Do I need it? Can I use less of it? Can it be used again? If the answer to all three is no, then make sure it goes in the right bin.

The bin you choose still matters

The same bag of waste can carry up to fifty times more carbon depending on which stream it goes into. Clean packaging in a clinical bag is the costliest mistake, in both carbon and money.

Roadmap

How the ratings work

  • Individual & team: you, your list or your theatre team can start without new infrastructure.
  • System changes: needs procurement, pharmacy, estates, IPC, catering or organisation-level decisions.
  • Easy wins: within your own practice or team, little or no cost, no approvals needed. Next up: needs a team agreement, a small purchase, or one other department on board. Longer-term: capital, estates or engineering, contracts, multi-stakeholder sign-off, or an assessment first.
  • ★ Big hitter: high climate impact per unit or per case (potent gases, or well-documented waste). This is an indicative flag; check it against your own usage data.
  • Ease ratings are a first-pass judgement. Re-sort anything that is easier or harder where you work.

Evidence key for case examples

Study peer-reviewed paper, audit or LCA. Service NHS or overseas health service case. Guidance policy, colleges, toolkits. CSH Centre for Sustainable Healthcare network (some pages need a free login). Trade vendor-authored or press; treat with caution.

For action leads: keep a log

One line per step is enough: date, action number, what was done, the result or impact, and the next step. Dates and numbers make the best evidence later.

Questions for each quarterly review

  • What moved since last review?
  • What is stuck, and who can unblock it?
  • Which easy win are we ready to extend to more lists or sites?
  • Which moderate action should move up next?
  • Any evidence to share (usage data, cost, carbon, staff feedback)?
  • Who else needs to hear about this?
