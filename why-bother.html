Why should I bother?

Because climate change is a health problem, and healthcare is part of the cause.

Hotter summers, dirtier air and floods make people ill. Hospitals then treat them, using lots of energy and stuff to do it. Operating theatres are some of the hungriest rooms in the building. Small changes here ripple outwards.

Sustainable healthcare is a triple win

Good sustainable care is usually just good care with less waste. When it is done well, three things get better at once.

PeopleBetter outcomes and experience for patients; less pointless work and more pride for staff.
PlanetLess carbon, less waste, cleaner air, for the community we serve now and our children later.
PoundsStuff we don't open, gas we don't waste and energy we don't use is money we keep for patient care.

Experts call this the "triple bottom line": judging care by its health, social, environmental and financial results together, not by cost alone.

The tap analogy. If the bath is overflowing, you don't start by buying more towels. You turn off the tap. Treating heat stroke and asthma is the towel. Using less energy, waste and gas is turning off the tap. Doing it in hospital also lowers our bills.

Explain it to someone else in one line: "The greenest care is good care that doesn't waste anything."

Additional reading

The evidence behind the numbers on this page.

The scale of the problem: more detail

The 2025 Lancet Countdown estimated about 546,000 heat-related deaths a year worldwide (2012–21 average), with the rate up 23% since the 1990s; it also linked 2.5 million deaths a year to air pollution from burning fossil fuels. The 2026 report recorded a new high of about 710,000 heat-linked deaths in 2023, more than 70% attributable to human-caused climate change.

In England, UKHSA counted 1,504 heat-associated deaths across summer 2025, and an interim 2,877 in two hot spells in May–June 2026 alone (final 2026 figures due early 2027). The previous whole-summer record was 2,985 in 2022.

The UK air pollution figure (29,000–43,000) is an "effect equivalent to" that many deaths, a modelled estimate rather than a count of death certificates.

The NHS and theatres: more detail

The NHS was the first health system to commit to net zero: 2040 for emissions it controls directly, 2045 for those it can influence (including the supply chain). These targets are in law through the Health and Care Act 2022. NHS Carbon Footprint emissions have fallen by over 68% since 1990.

The "3 to 6 times" theatre figure is about energy intensity, mostly from heating, ventilation and air conditioning, measured in three large hospitals (UK, US, Canada). It is not a claim that theatres produce 3–6 times the hospital's total carbon. Theatres are also often quoted as producing 20–30% of hospital waste.
