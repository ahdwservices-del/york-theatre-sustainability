Early findings

Low flow, less waste

What our anaesthetic machines recorded about sevoflurane use, January to November 2026.

Where we are now

Our current practice, which we need to improve.

57%of our sevoflurane goes to the scavenger, not the patient.*Target: 50% or less
2.0 L/minour usual maintenance fresh gas flow, in every area.Target: below 1, aim for 0.5
1 in 3sevoflurane cases never reach 1 L/min.Target: fewer than 1 in 7

What can I do?

1

Go low early

  • Preoxygenate: vaporiser off, tight seal, 6 L/min (10 in pregnancy) to end-tidal O2 of 90% or more.
  • Induce: mask-ventilate at 6 L/min; reach for an adjunct before more flow.
  • Intubate: turn the flow down while the circuit is open.
  • Airway secured: 1 L/min within 5 minutes, then 0.5 L/min.
2

Vaporiser to 0 when suturing starts

Keep flow low while the surgeon closes. When surgery ends, raise flow to the patient's minute ventilation: 15 L/min does not wake them faster.

3

Induce on the machine's circle system, not a Mapleson D

A Mapleson D induction uses about 19 mL of sevoflurane, more than half a whole case's worth, and nearly all of it goes to the scavenger.

If we all do this, every year we save£19–26k17–24 tonnes CO2e

What does 24 tonnes look like?

3½ times round the world145,000 km in a petrol car

Tips

Need to deepen? Burst, then back

A minute or two of high flow, then straight back down.

Almost all sevoflurane ends up in the atmosphere

Very little is metabolised. What the patient absorbs is breathed out in recovery, so the only fix is to use less.

Save more carbon: regional or TIVA

For the same operation: spinal 5.5 kg CO2e, TIVA 6.6 kg, low-flow sevoflurane 10.8 kg.
